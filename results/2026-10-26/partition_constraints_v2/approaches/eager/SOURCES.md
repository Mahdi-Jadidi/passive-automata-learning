# Sources checked by Fork A

Access date: 2026-10-26. Sources below are primary. Checked statements are
identified narrowly; this is not an exhaustive novelty search.

1. Léo Henry, Mohammad Reza Mousavi, Thomas Neele and Matteo Sammartino,
   *A Detailed Account of Compositional Automata Learning through Alphabet
   Refinement*, arXiv:2504.16624v3, revised 1 April 2026.
   [Full primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16624v3).
   Checked Section 4.1 Proposition 5 and Section 6.1 Corollary 12 and the
   counterexample definition between them. These give the finite observation
   consistency antecedent. The complete algorithm is active and allows
   overlapping alphabet distributions. Our two-block criterion is a
   specialization, not a novelty claim. Only these relevant sections and the
   model description were checked for this task; the previous cycle's broader
   review remains in baseline/LITERATURE_REVIEW.md.

2. Pascal Baumann, Moses Ganardi, Ramanathan S. Thinniyam and Georg Zetzsche,
   *Existential Definability over the Subword Ordering*, STACS 2022,
   LIPIcs 219, 7:1–7:15, DOI 10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2022.7.
   [Primary record](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2022.7),
   [primary PDF](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol219-stacs2022/LIPIcs.STACS.2022.7/LIPIcs.STACS.2022.7.pdf).
   Checked metadata and Lemma 3.11 on printed page 7:10: binary projections
   determine a word on alphabets of size at least two. Our proof states the
   singleton boundary explicitly because a candidate block can be unary.
   This lemma is existing combinatorics on words, not an original finding.

The eager encoding is an explicit application of these ingredients to C002's
fixed ownership model. This limited source check does not establish that the
encoding or lazy refinement is previously unpublished. No novelty claim is
made for either approach, and this branch's initial checks establish no
performance result.
