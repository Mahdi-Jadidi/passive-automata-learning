## Singleton to q=2 boundary (our direct example, not a sourced novelty claim)

On alphabet {a,b,c}, positives abc,bac fit factors B1={a,b}, B2={c} with L1={ab,ba}, L2={c}. Their a,b binary projections differ, so S001 builds opposition ab, although a,b truly belong to the same block. Thus the singleton opposition rule ceases to be a sound different-block constraint when one local language may have two words. Even equality of per-letter counts does not rescue it. With positives ab,aab on a local block, singleton count inconsistency likewise ceases to imply infeasibility for q=2. These examples are proposed Stage2 tests, not claimed executed until captured. A sound bounded-language provider must count distinct full-block positive projections and check negative rejection, rather than reuse singleton opposition as a hard constraint.
