# Independence contract — V34 proof gate

Scope: design and mathematical proof for two exact candidate engines, logic and hypergraph; no solver implementation, tests or benchmarks before independent approval. This contract distinguishes a known formulation, an independently implemented algorithm, and a novelty claim. None implies the next. No novelty or performance claim follows from the gate.

Input: declared ordered finite alphabet of opaque atomic nonempty IDs, finite positive and negative full words including epsilon, with unknown number of nonempty disjoint blocks and unbounded regular factors combined by AND. Unused declared IDs remain. Normalize duplicate words by first occurrence; validate schema and alphabet membership explicitly. For B, H(B) is the set of positive projections and R(B) the negatives whose projection is absent from H(B). A partition is compatible iff the union of R(B) over its blocks covers N. Monotonicity under enlargement proves coarsening preserves compatibility. A label contradiction makes the instance infeasible. Empty alphabet has the single empty partition, compatible iff N is empty. With nonempty alphabet, empty P or empty N makes all partitions compatible. Exact sample consistency is not physical ownership.

A proposed solver must be a fundamental exact engine with its own solving algorithm and representation. It may not import, invoke, dispatch to, or fall back to the existing reference, RGS enumerator, subset DP, graph or hybrid solving engines. Existing reference solvers may be used later only as explicitly identified test oracles. A separate validation/certificate checker is permitted if its dependencies are declared and it does not secretly serve as the solver. An external general-purpose solver, if proposed, must be named with exact role, dependency and certificate assumptions; a formulation alone is not an executed independent algorithm. No heuristics as the claimed exact method.

Initial output contract is ONE optimal compatible partition plus independently checkable feasibility and optimality certificate, or a checkable infeasibility certificate; specify how such certificates are represented, produced and verified, including trusted verifier assumptions, finite termination, interruption, unknown and invalid input. Objective is maximum block count, unknown K. A feasible witness alone gives a lower bound, not an optimality certificate. Outputs C (all compatible partitions), O (optima), canonical first two witnesses and deterministic factor/rejection witnesses are separate stronger obligations. Compare candidate runtimes only under the same fully specified output contract and preprocessing/verification accounting. If no practical certificate format or verifier is established, mark that guarantee OPEN rather than infer it.

Proof must demonstrate mapping in both directions and objective preservation, including empty/epsilon cases and atomic IDs. Account for representation size, construction, solving and certificate verification costs; NP-completeness does not prohibit finite exact methods. No bounded-state assumption, fixed K, physical uniqueness, unproved generative model, hidden uniqueness or universal speed claim is allowed. Unknown/timeouts are not infeasible. Biggest overlooked issue: an encoding that finds a valid partition is not automatically a provably optimal independent algorithm, a counting algorithm, or a physical-identification procedure.
