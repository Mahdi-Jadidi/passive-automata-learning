No novelty claim for sparse coloring, forced coloring, trace projection or decomposition. These notes distinguish what was actually retrieved from stronger unstated claims.

1. `Uniquely colorable graphs`, Journal of Combinatorial Theory 6(3), 1969, pp.264-270, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/S0021-9800(69)80086-4 . Publisher search result https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021980069800864 supplied the definition and abstract including uniquely n-colorable examples without K_n. Direct open returned HTTP403. Therefore full-paper proof/details were not inspected; our elementary anchor induction is supplied independently. This abstract is enough to warn against claiming completeness for a clique-based certificate. Search query: `site.sciencedirect.com uniquely colorable graphs Harary Cartwright 1968`; also returned unrelated 1968 enumeration work, which is not used.

2. Bela Bollobas, `Uniquely colorable graphs`, JCT Series B25(1),1978,54-61. Primary author-publication repository https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/facpubs/6063/ retrieved fully; DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/S0095-8956(78)80010-0 . Abstract defines uniqueness up to partitions and gives sufficient degree-based results. No reliance on that paper for our proof or novelty claim; sparse known-k forcing is a restricted sufficient condition, not characterization. Only abstract/metadata reviewed.

3. M Mukund and M Sohoni, `Gossiping, Asynchronous Automata and Zielonka's Theorem`, Report TCS-94-2, 1994, author institution page https://www.imsc.res.in/~madhavan/papers/tcs-94-2.html retrieved fully. Its abstract constructs asynchronous automata recognizing a given recognizable trace language over a given independence alphabet. This concerns synthesis under an established independence structure; it does not by itself establish passive inference of an unknown alphabet partition from finite labels. The current project uses disjoint independent factor products, a simpler semantic class than general synchronized asynchronous automata. No borrowed theorem identifies the hidden partition here.
