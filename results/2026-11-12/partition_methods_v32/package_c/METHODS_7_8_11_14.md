# Method dossiers: 7, 8, 11–14

Model: ordered finite atomic alphabet Σ; finite positive and negative full-word samples P,N; independent regular factors over disjoint nonempty ownership blocks, with conjunctive acceptance. H(B)={π_B(p):p∈P}, R(B)={n∈N:π_B(n)∉H(B)}. A partition is compatible iff its blocks' R sets cover N. R is monotone with block inclusion, so coarsening preserves compatibility. Unknown K requires C (all compatible partitions), k* (maximum blocks), O (optimal ties), first two canonical RGS witnesses for both families and deterministic factor/rejection witnesses; retain validation, arbitrary-precision counts, replay, and interruption contract. Duplicate labels are normalized; contradictory labels imply no compatible partition. ε is a word. For Σ=∅, the sole empty partition is compatible iff N=∅. These are sample-consistency claims, not physical recovery.

## 7. Agglomerative merging

Start with singletons and repeatedly merge two blocks, choosing e.g. maximum additional negative coverage. Stop at the first compatible partition. Replay verifies the output partition and R coverage, and H(B) provides regular factors; coarsenings remain compatible. This establishes feasibility only, not optimality or counts.

Counterexample: Σ={a,b,c}, P={ε,abc}, N={a}. The singleton partition is incompatible; ab|c and ac|b are compatible, as is abc, but a|bc is not. A merge ordering that merges b,c first forces the next merge to reach one-block abc, although two-block optima exist; this does not refute the particular maximum-additional-coverage rule, which chooses ab or ac here. An exact variant branches over all merges, deduplicates partitions (many merge histories represent one partition), and checks and aggregates the complete family, with canonical witness ordering and replay. This terminates but has no claimed polynomial bound. An independent exact count and optimality certificate could instead certify a proposed result. Biggest overlooked issue: counting merge histories as partitions overcounts, and stopping at first compatibility loses compatible alternatives.

## 8. Divisive splitting

Start at one block and recursively split one block into two nonempty blocks, rejecting an incompatible split and all its refinements. For noncontradictory samples and Σ nonempty the root is compatible. Every compatible target Π is reachable through compatible splits: split its target blocks off successively; every intermediate partition coarsens Π. Pruning is sound because a compatible refinement would imply its coarsening is compatible. Complete traversal with canonical parent or partition deduplication, full count aggregation, global RGS ordering, and replay is exact; no polynomial bound follows.

The example above shows that trying only a|bc and stopping after failure misses both compatible two-block partitions. Any claim that a locally maximal compatible partition is globally maximum requires a separate proof. Contradictory labels and the empty alphabet need separate root handling. Biggest overlooked issue: sound pruning of one failed branch does not justify stopping after that branch, and different split paths duplicate partitions.

## 11. Local search and randomness

Propose partitions via moves, merges, splits, or random RGSs; score coverage and block count, retaining candidates checked by projection replay. A compatible candidate proves k* is at least its block count, not an upper bound, C, O, or ordered witnesses. Random seeds give reproducibility, not coverage. With independent sampling from a nontrivial distribution giving each partition positive probability, a finite run has positive probability of omitting any particular partition. Exhaustive support traversal with exact deduplication and completion proof is enumeration; an independent upper-bound, model-count, and order certificate can instead certify a candidate.

On the preceding instance, a run proposing only abc finds a feasible but suboptimal partition. Even finding an optimum does not determine ties or total count. No sample distribution is assumed. Biggest overlooked issue: high success probability under an assumed distribution cannot prove exact counts for the particular instance.

## 12. Automata first, decompose later

Infer a DFA A consistent with P,N, then inspect transitions or product decompositions for candidate blocks. For disjoint P,N, the finite language P is one consistent regular-language witness. Selecting one consistent language changes the search object: it need not represent every sample-compatible partition. Even minimum-state inference does not supply the entire partition family.

For Σ={a,b}, P={ε}, N=∅, the one-state language Σ* is consistent and has one-block and singleton-factor realizations. The sample-compatible count is 2, optimum 2 with singleton partition; selecting a one-block realization cannot determine ownership or the requested family. Further, Σ* has distinct one-block and singleton-factor realizations even with complete language knowledge. Use inference as a candidate generator followed by a complete solver for the original sample, or specify a changed fixed-language/bounded-state problem and prove equivalence separately. Empty alphabet with N=∅ accepts ε by the empty conjunction. Biggest overlooked issue: automaton states and symbol ownership blocks are different notions, and choosing a language loses other sample-consistent languages. Angluin's teacher model learns regular languages, not this partition family [1].

## 13. Statistical features and clustering

Construct features such as trace presence, projected lengths, or co-occurrence, cluster symbols into blocks, then replay compatibility. Hierarchical agglomerative graph clustering is a well-defined family for edge-weighted graphs [2], but its results do not supply the partition-family guarantees here. No sampling distribution, stochastic independence, separation, metric, or known cluster count is specified, so these scores do not establish compatibility, optimality, exact counts, or physical ownership. Raw duplicate frequencies can change while normalized sample compatibility stays fixed.

On Σ={a,b,c}, P={ε,abc}, N={a}, positive-trace co-occurrence is identical for all pairs; selecting bc first and following that merge path yields abc rather than the two-block solutions ab|c and ac|b. This refutes that specific tie-dependent procedure, not all clustering procedures. Σ* also permits different physical partitions even with complete membership information. Clustering can guide a complete exact search or be justified by explicitly added generative/separation assumptions and a relevant proof; such assumptions restrict or change the model. Biggest overlooked issue: pairwise associations do not encode a disjunction of alternative rejecting blocks, and a predictive clustering is not a family-count proof.

## 14. Active queries and structural assumptions

A membership oracle can label selected full words; add them to P or N and solve the enlarged finite sample. Each answer changes the compatibility family, so new counts are not automatically the original counts. A global-language equivalence oracle supplying counterexamples is an additional teacher assumption [1]. Even perfect membership/equivalence information for Σ* cannot distinguish its one-block and singleton-factor realizations; global language identification does not universally identify physical ownership.

Structural restrictions: (i) bounding block size d changes the family unless omitted blocks are proved irrelevant; (ii) a supplied true partition does not yield the original all-family counts; (iii) a promised admissible-block list allows exact cover only after proving it contains every block appearing in a compatible partition; (iv) singleton compatibility is a checkable certificate in the original model: by coarsening, every partition is compatible, C=Bell(m), k*=m, O=1, and canonical witnesses follow from RGS order. The last includes m=0 when N=∅, subject to the normal contradiction and validation handling. A uniqueness promise needs a precise independent justification; unique optimum does not imply unique compatible partition. None of these observations proves a general polynomial full-contract algorithm. Biggest overlooked issue: learning a language need not determine its decomposition, while active labels change the finite dataset whose counts were requested.

## Completion, cost, and claim-status addendum

The earlier independent review in `../package_a/REVIEW_C.md` applies to its recorded hash, not automatically to this addendum. Statuses below concern the precise claims here, not all algorithms in a family.

- **7 merge — PROVED** for exhaustive traversal with duplicate control and complete aggregation; **REFUTED-SPECIFIC** for the indicated bc-first stopping rule. At most Bell(m) distinct partitions are stored; naive outgoing pair merges cost O(m²) per visited partition, besides encoding and projection checks. A greedy path has at most m−1 merges, but its scoring and replay still cost time in the explicit sample length.
- **8 split — PROVED** for complete traversal and the stated incompatible-branch pruning; **REFUTED-SPECIFIC** for stopping after the single failed split. A block of size s has 2^(s−1)−1 unordered nontrivial bipartitions, so generating successors can itself be exponential. Deduplicated state storage can reach Bell(m); pruning does not imply a polynomial worst-case bound.
- **11 random/local — PROVED** for replayed feasibility and complete finite-support enumeration; **REFUTED-SPECIFIC** for the cited finite run as an optimality method. For independent draws with positive probability for every one of finitely many partitions, every partition is drawn eventually with probability one. Almost-sure eventual coverage does not itself identify the time of coverage: a terminating exact procedure must track distinct partitions and certify that all Bell(m) have appeared, or provide an equivalent completeness certificate. Until then, a finite run is incomplete, even if it happened to see all solutions. The representation needs O(m) labels per partition and may require Bell(m) storage; expected sampling time depends on the smallest sampling probability and is not uniformly polynomial.
- **12 automata-first — REFUTED-SPECIFIC** as a claim that selecting one model or realization recovers the family; **PROVED** only when followed by a complete original-instance partition search or an independently verified full certificate. Minimum DFA, arbitrary DFA, finite-language factor construction, and product decomposition are different selection rules; no unspecified choice identifies the partition family. Candidate inference/decomposition costs depend on the chosen model and state representation; no bound on candidate state count is supplied. Even a verified automaton needs the cost of a separate complete family search to establish C, O, and canonical witnesses.
- **13 clustering — REFUTED-SPECIFIC** for the stated co-occurrence tie rule; **OPEN** for any proposed statistical recovery theorem until its sampling law, identifiability assumptions, and proof are specified. Computing a feature matrix may cost O(m²) space plus a scan of explicit traces, but clustering cost depends on feature and linkage; this omits the potentially Bell(m)-scale exact completion. Identifiability fails without added assumptions even with complete global-language access: Σ* has distinct ownership realizations. No probability of correct recovery follows from the finite-sample model.
- **14 active/structural — PROVED** for the singleton certificate and for exact completion after explicitly incorporating oracle answers; **OPEN** for physical recovery under any unprovided additional structural assumptions. Query cost and answer length, structural-promise validation, and the cost of solving the enlarged instance all count; an oracle is not free input. The original sample's counts require solving or certifying the original sample itself, not silently substituting augmented data.

For a direct replay with k≤m blocks, filter each explicit word per block and compare each negative projection to positive projections; a straightforward implementation uses polynomial time in k, sample cardinalities and total word length, with an O(k |N| |P| L) coarse bound if L bounds individual word length, plus O(k(|P|+|N|)L) projection construction. It uses exact atomic-ID equality. Cached sets or hashing change practical costs and need collision-safe equality, not the proof. All complete methods must separately report infeasibility versus interruption; output C=O=0 and no optimum/witness when infeasible, and never present partial counts as exact. For feasible inputs with fewer than two eligible witnesses, report only those that exist. Empty P makes each existing block reject every negative; empty N makes every partition compatible; unused IDs remain in Σ and participate in partitions, including singleton blocks. With Σ=∅, N≠∅ is infeasible even when P=∅ because there is no block to reject a negative.
